Volume I

The Network

River One · deep learning and neural networks

River One learns by being shown. You give it a picture and the right label, it guesses, you measure how wrong the guess was, and every adjustable number in the system moves a small step in the direction that would have made it less wrong. Repeat a few billion times. That is the whole river, and the volume follows it from a 1943 drawing of a neuron to a model that predicts the next word of the internet.

The teaching spine is the first seven chapters of 3Blue1Brown’s neural-network series: what a network is, gradient descent, backpropagation in words and in calculus, then the Transformer, attention, and where facts live inside the layers. Welch Labs supplies the second voice for the hardware and history, the perceptron in particular. Talking Nets supplies the method for the people: let each one tell it in their own voice, verse by verse.

I added one track the videos skip. 3Blue1Brown teaches with squared error; every modern language model is trained on cross-entropy, so it gets its own song.

Volume II

The Reward

River Two · reinforcement learning

River Two learns by being told the score. Nobody gives it the right answer. It acts, the world responds, now and then a reward arrives, and it has to work out which of its many earlier moves earned it. That is the credit assignment problem, and the volume follows the answers to it from cats in boxes to a cell in a monkey’s brain that turns out to be running the same equation as a computer program.

The teaching spine is David Silver’s 2015 UCL lectures, with Sutton and Barto’s textbook behind them. Silver builds the subject in a strict order: what a decision problem is, Bellman, then learning from experience, then the actor and the critic. The songs keep that order so each one stands on the last.

The early history is tangled with River One, and that tangle is useful. Shannon and Minsky appear in both volumes because the 1950s machines learned from reward and were built from artificial neurons. The track on that decade shows the rivers before they separate.

Volume III

The Confluence

Deep reinforcement learning, then post-training and the Chinese labs

The rivers meet when a neural network becomes the function inside a reinforcement learner. River One supplies the network that can read pixels or text; River Two supplies the way to learn from a score. DeepMind joins them on Atari in 2013 and on Go in 2016. Then the same pairing gets turned on language models, and the subject becomes post-training: teaching a model that knows everything how to behave.

The teaching spine is Nathan Lambert’s RLHF book, which covers far more than human feedback: preference data, reward models, the leash that keeps a tuned model close to where it started, verifiable rewards, and the reasoning models. From there the volume follows the work that has moved fastest since 2025, much of it published by Chinese labs, and ends with the race.

Three tracks here are about economics and people: the doing-more-with-less engineering, the labs themselves, and the race between them and the US frontier labs. The figures in those tracks are current to mid-2026 and are the ones to refresh before you play them to anyone.

Aha

Song · Mandopop R&B

In the roomGRPO and DeepSeek-R1: sample a group of answers to the same question, score each one, and reward answers for beating the group average. The group replaces the critic, which saves memory and compute.

III.6

Many Teachers

Song · Afrobeat

In the roomDistillation from first principles to MOPD. A teacher’s full spread of probabilities carries more than its top answer. Off-policy distillation copies the teacher’s finished answers. On-policy distillation lets the student write and has the teacher grade every word. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation uses a separate specialist teacher per skill, each trained by RL, so one student inherits all of them.

III.7

In the roomHow the Chinese labs do more with less. Mixture of experts wakes only a few specialists per word. Compressed and sparse attention stops the model holding every earlier word in memory at full size. Lower-precision numbers halve and halve again the memory and arithmetic. Multi-token prediction drafts several words at once. The arrangement is the argument: two instruments.
