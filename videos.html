Two Rivers

A song cycle in three acts about how AI was built, and the people who built it: thirty-three songs, each with its own video.

The structure is the argument. Two rivers rise from the same spring. River One learns by being shown the answer: neural networks, Volume I. River Two learns by being told the score: reinforcement learning, Volume II. For half a century they run side by side, and in Volume III they meet. The joined river is how today's AI is made. An overture draws the map, and a finale carries it out to the sea.

On River One: a runaway teenager hiding in a library, writing to Bertrand Russell about the mistakes in his book, who ends up drawing the first artificial neuron at a doctor's kitchen table. Frank Rosenblatt, whose machine the Navy promised would walk, talk, see and write. Geoffrey Hinton keeping the idea alive through the long winter, two graphics cards overheating a student's bedroom, and a bidding war for three academics in a Lake Tahoe casino.

On River Two: Thorndike's cats escaping puzzle boxes, chickens living in William James's cellar, Claude Shannon's mechanical mouse learning a maze, and a single cell in a monkey's brain that fires for juice it didn't expect. Where they meet: Lee Sedol at the board for Move 37, the quant in Hangzhou who paid for DeepSeek himself, and one German professor, footnoting all of it. Watch and listen →
